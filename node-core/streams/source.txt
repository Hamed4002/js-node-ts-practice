The Eternal Wanderer

In the age before memory, when the oceans were still nameless and the stars whispered only to themselves, there existed a wanderer who had no beginning and no end. His name had been lost to time, for he had worn countless faces and walked beneath skies that had long since turned to dust. He was called The Eternal Wanderer, a soul untethered from the threads of destiny, doomed—or perhaps blessed—to roam the infinite tapestry of existence.

He did not walk upon ordinary paths. His footsteps echoed through the corridors of forgotten realities, through the ruins of civilizations that never were, and across the dreams of gods who had fallen asleep before creation itself began. Each world he visited was both a memory and a prophecy, a reflection of all that could be and all that had already vanished.

The Wanderer carried with him a lantern, dim and flickering, that held a single spark of the First Fire—the original light that had birthed the universe. It was said that as long as that flame remained lit, hope itself would never truly die. Yet the flame grew weaker with every century that passed, and the Wanderer feared that one day it would fade, plunging all of existence into silence.

The First World

He came first to a world of endless twilight, where mountains floated in the air and rivers flowed upward into the clouds. The people there spoke in songs instead of words, their voices harmonizing with the wind. They welcomed the Wanderer, offering him fruit that glowed like the moon and water that shimmered like silver.

But he could not stay. For though the people smiled, their eyes were hollow; they were bound by the illusion of eternal peace, unable to feel sorrow, joy, or longing. Their hearts were still, their dreams static. “Without the ache of imperfection,” the Wanderer whispered to himself, “beauty is nothing more than a mirror reflecting nothing.”

And so he left them behind, his lantern burning a little dimmer.

The Second World

Next, he descended into a realm made entirely of time. Every stone, every leaf, every gust of wind was an echo of the past repeating itself. Here, history never ended—it looped endlessly, like a song that refused to conclude. The people of this world worshipped clocks, believing that precision was divinity and that by mastering the rhythm of the seconds, they could defy eternity.

The Wanderer stayed for many ages, studying their craft, until he realized their curse: they had become prisoners of their own perfection. Nothing ever changed; no mistakes were allowed, no chaos permitted. Life had frozen beneath the weight of flawless order.

And so he shattered the greatest clock of their world, releasing chaos once again. The people screamed, yet in their terror was rebirth. “Only through imperfection,” he said as he departed, “can creation breathe.”

The Third World

His next journey led him into a desert of glass. Each grain of sand reflected his face—millions of him staring back, each a different version of what he could have been. One was a king, another a beggar, another a child who had never left his home. The desert stretched endlessly, and the Wanderer realized he was walking through the landscape of his own possibilities.

He walked for a thousand years, perhaps a thousand lives. He saw every choice he had ever made, and every choice he had refused to make. And somewhere in that vast desert of mirrors, he began to forget which reflection was truly his.

But the lantern called to him—a small, trembling light in the sea of glass—and he remembered: the flame was not his to keep, but his to protect. With trembling hands, he lifted the lantern once again and walked onward.

The Meeting with the Watcher

One night, beneath a sky of burning constellations, the Wanderer met a being known as The Watcher of Ends. The Watcher was older than time, with eyes like collapsing stars and a voice that trembled between thunder and silence.

“Why do you carry that dying flame?” the Watcher asked. “The universe will end, whether your light endures or not.”

The Wanderer looked up. “Because even if it ends,” he said softly, “it deserves to end with light.”

The Watcher laughed—a sound like the cracking of ice on a distant world—and disappeared. Yet when the Wanderer looked at his lantern, the flame had grown stronger.

The Last World

At last, after eons beyond counting, the Wanderer reached a world unlike any other—a place where every sound was a memory, every shadow a forgotten story. Here he found a great tree whose roots pierced through all realities, binding them together. Its leaves shimmered with every color ever seen and every color yet to be imagined.

The Wanderer placed his lantern at the base of the tree, and the flame rose high, merging with the roots. Light spread through every branch, every leaf, every world that had ever been born. For the first time since the dawn of time, the Wanderer smiled.

And then he vanished—not in death, but in return. He became part of the flame, part of the tree, part of everything that ever was and ever would be.

They say that sometimes, in the quiet between worlds, a faint light still flickers—a reminder that no journey is ever truly over, and no darkness ever truly eternal.
